Card 1

Before the first shot

ModeAvEV±0 → decideQualityJPEG
  1. Turn the mode dial to Av. Auto hid every choice below.
  2. Look at the light and decide on exposure compensation now (card 4).
  3. Subject within arm's length? Set the focus area to a single small point (card 2).
  4. Leave quality on JPEG unless the light is harsh (card 6).
All 171 shots were full Auto: EV 0 every time, focus picked from the whole frame.
Card 2 · Blur

Up close, under 1.5 m

Food, kids at the table, pets, details.

ModeAvAperturef/4–5.6AF areaSpot
  • AF area: Spot AF on the R50, 1-point AF on the M50. Put it on the eye or the edge that matters.
  • Stop down to f/4, or f/5.6 under 1 m.
  • Close and dim? Take a step back to about 1.5 m and shoot f/2.8, or stay close at f/4 and accept the higher ISO. Sharp and grainy beats clean and blurry.
27 of your 31 blurry shots were focused closer than 1.5 m, mostly at f/2.8.

Card 3 · Noise

Dim room

Restaurants, living room at night, indoor events.

ModeAvLens35mmAperturef/1.8
  • Put on the 35mm f/1.8 and open it all the way to f/1.8.
  • Let ISO float. Opening up is what brings it down.
  • Subject under 1.5 m? Card 2 wins: focus first.
34 shots were ISO 2000 or higher, up to 6400, while Auto kept the 35mm at f/2.8. Going from f/2.8 to f/1.8 is 1.3 stops more light: ISO 6400 becomes about 2500.
Card 4 · Too dark or blown out

Tricky light

Window behind someone, snow or sky, a spotlit face on a dark stage.

Bright behind+2/3…+1Dark / spotlit−2/3…−1
  • Bright background making your subject a silhouette: dial plus.
  • Mostly dark scene, or a bright subject on black: dial minus.
  • Review the shot with highlight alert on. Blinking areas are gone for good in a JPEG: dial down a third and reshoot.
  • Set it back to ±0 when you move on.
35 shots came out dark and 27 blown out. Exposure compensation was 0 on all of them, and JPEG left nothing to recover.
Card 5

People

Anyone who can blink, turn or talk.

Frames2–3AFon the eye
  • Take 2 to 3 frames of every moment, not one.
  • Keep the AF point on the nearer eye (card 2 if close).
From the postmortem's habit list. A single frame of a person leaves no backup for a blink or a focus miss.
Card 6

Hard light only: RAW

Midday sun, strong backlight, mixed stage or neon light.

QualityRAW+JPEGAfterback to JPEG
  • Switch to RAW (or RAW+JPEG) for that scene only.
  • Switch back when the light gets easy again.
RAW can rescue the dark and blown shots from card 4, but it costs several times the space.

Where this comes from: the Nov 20 camera-settings postmortem of 171 shots (158 R50, Nov 10–18; 13 M50, Nov 17–18). It read EXIF and pixel statistics only, so the cause behind each miss is a reading of the numbers, not a visual check.

Menu names can differ slightly by firmware; check each one on the camera once. The depth-of-field table is calculated, not measured.
